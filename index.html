FORMULA3

Framework for Optimal Resource Management Using GeneraLizable AI in FR3 Dynamic Programmable Wireless Environments

Self-adapting FR3 networks that combine learning, sensing, and programmable environments to deliver reliable, high-throughput, low-latency 6G connectivity.

Who benefits

Applications that cannot wait for a dropped link

Emerging 6G applications need consistent multi-Gbps throughput and sub-millisecond latency indoors, not just good peak numbers.

Immersive AR and VR

Needs: multi-Gbps throughput with sub-millisecond latency. A stalled frame breaks immersion.

FORMULA3: steers beams and surfaces ahead of the user, so movement and blockage don't interrupt the stream.

Remote healthcare

Needs: links that stay up, without surprises, for remote monitoring and tele-surgery.

FORMULA3: forecasts outages before they happen and reroutes through surfaces. The team's earlier work forecast outages about 100 ms ahead.

Advanced manufacturing

Needs: reliable wireless in crowded spaces where machines, people, and layouts keep changing.

FORMULA3: senses occupancy and layout changes from the channel itself, and adapts its AI models when the floor is rearranged.

The question

How can FR3 (7–24 GHz) programmable wireless environments, built from RIS and metallic reflector arrays, keep communication fast and reliable as people move, rooms fill, and furniture shifts?

< 7 GHz Sub-6 GHz Good coverage, limited bandwidth
7–24 GHz FR3 upper mid-band A balance of bandwidth and coverage for 6G
> 24 GHz mmWave Very wide bandwidth, fragile links

~10× more bandwidth than sub-6 GHz

~2× the range of mmWave, with better wall penetration

80%+ of mobile data is consumed indoors, where FORMULA3 focuses

Approach

Measure. Model. Predict. Adapt.

Reacting to a lost link is too late for AR/VR or tele-surgery. FORMULA3 makes the network proactive. It learns from real FR3 measurements, predicts outages before they happen, and reconfigures surfaces using context it senses from the channel itself.

  1. 01

    Measure

    Characterize dynamic FR3 channels on new hybrid RIS and reflector-array testbeds, indoors and outdoor-to-indoor.

  2. 02

    Model

    Build validated channel models and generate rich datasets of mobility, occupancy, and layout dynamics.

  3. 03

    Predict

    Use physics-informed AI to forecast channel gains and outages before they disrupt service.

  4. 04

    Adapt

    Reconfigure surfaces and reassign users using context sensed from the channel (ISAC), and keep learning as rooms change.

Every step is validated against measurements and simulation in a unified evaluation platform.

Research thrusts

Four integrated thrusts

Measurements feed the models, the models train the AI, and context sensing keeps the AI accurate in rooms it has never seen.

T1

Channel characterization & dataset generation

A first-of-its-kind hybrid FR3 testbed with fabricated RIS panels and metallic reflector arrays, plus a calibrated simulation suite for dynamic data.

AI-native proactive resource management

Physics-informed AI that predicts surface channels, excludes shadowed elements, and configures surfaces to suppress LoS/NLoS interference.

Generalization via ISAC & continual learning

Sensing occupancy, mobility, and layout from the channel itself to pick the right model, and retraining when the environment drifts.

What we are aiming for

From today's limits to FORMULA3's targets

Where conventional approaches fall short today, and what the project sets out to achieve.

AI facing conditions it wasn't trained on

Today>50%performance loss
→
Target<2%QoS degradation

Sensing occupancy, mobility, and layout from the channel picks the right model; continual learning handles rearranged rooms.

Signaling overhead

TodayAllsurface elements managed
→
Target−50%overhead

Shadowed RIS tiles and reflector elements are excluded instead of reconfigured, so the network spends effort only where it helps.

Channel prediction with few sensors

TodayHigherror from sparse data
→
Target104×lower error

Physics-informed AI builds propagation knowledge into training and densifies sparse channel data.

Interference between direct and reflected paths

TodayCappedQoS from LoS/NLoS interference
→
Target10×better QoS

Users are assigned to surfaces, and RIS, SIM, and MRA settings are chosen so direct and reflected signals stop canceling each other.

"Today" reflects conventional approaches and the team's earlier studies, mostly at mmWave and in optical wireless. Targets are research goals set at the start of the project, not measured results; results will be reported in publications.
