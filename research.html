The challenge

Great spectrum, unpredictable rooms

The upper mid-band (FR3, 7–24 GHz) offers hundreds of MHz of spectrum and penetrates walls better than mmWave. That makes it attractive for indoor and outdoor-to-indoor (O2I) 6G services such as immersive AR/VR, tele-surgery, and holographic communication. FR3 links can still be blocked by people, furniture, and changes to a room's layout.

Programmable wireless environments (PWEs) address this by placing controllable surfaces on walls to steer signals around obstacles. But most current control is reactive. It responds after a link fails, which causes latency spikes and throughput drops that emerging applications cannot tolerate.

What dynamics do to channels

  • General channel models break down. In our earlier mmWave studies, fitted statistical models were off by 12–18% once users moved and rooms changed. Similar effects are expected in FR3.
  • Channels become sparse. Outages come and go over time, and many surface elements are shadowed at any given moment.
  • Channels are non-stationary. Channel statistics shift with occupancy, with each user's mobility stage (entering, wandering, leaving), and with the room's layout.
  • So AI struggles to generalize. In our tests, conventional models lost more than 50% of their performance when conditions differed from their training data.

The FORMULA3 approach

Proactive, physics-informed, and context-aware

FORMULA3 is a Framework for Optimal Resource Management Using GeneraLizable AI in FR3 dynamic PWEs. It rests on three ideas.

01 · Proactive control

Act before the outage

Anticipate changes in the channel and adjust the network before a link fails, instead of reacting afterward.

02 · Physics-informed AI

Learn with the physics

Combine knowledge of how radio waves propagate with machine learning, so the AI stays reliable.

03 · ISAC context

Sense the room, then decide

Use the wireless signal itself to understand what is happening in the room, and let that context guide the network.

Hybrid surfaces in FR3

FORMULA3 combines three kinds of programmable surfaces whose strengths complement each other, balancing passive and active control and adapting to line-of-sight (LoS) and non-line-of-sight (NLoS) transitions.

RISReconfigurable intelligent surfaces: nearly passive beam steering with low power use.
SIMStacked intelligent metasurfaces: multi-layer, active wavefront shaping and beamforming.
MRAMetallic (Fresnel) reflector arrays: low cost and versatile across frequencies.

VINES research vectors

Where FORMULA3 fits in VINES

VINES (Verticals-enabling Intelligent Network Systems) is a joint program of NSF and the Research Council of Finland. FORMULA3 addresses three of its research vectors.

Glossary

Terms used on this site

FR3Frequency Range 3
The 7–24 GHz upper mid-band, between today's sub-7 GHz and mmWave bands, and a candidate band for 6G.
PWEProgrammable wireless environment
A space whose walls carry controllable surfaces, so the network can shape how radio waves travel.
RISReconfigurable intelligent surface
A panel of tiny, tunable elements that redirects incoming signals with very little power.
SIMStacked intelligent metasurface
Several metasurface layers stacked to shape wavefronts and form beams directly in the wave domain.
MRAMetallic reflector array
An array of metal (Fresnel) reflectors. Low cost, and it works across a wide range of frequencies.
ISACIntegrated sensing and communication
Using the communication signal itself to sense the environment, such as how many people are present and how they move.
O2IOutdoor-to-indoor
Links from an outdoor transmitter to users inside a building.
LoS / NLoS(Non-)line-of-sight
Whether there is a direct path between transmitter and receiver, or only reflected paths.
QoSQuality of service
The throughput, latency, and reliability an application actually experiences.
Concept drift
When the data an AI model sees in operation changes from the data it was trained on, for example after a room is rearranged.
